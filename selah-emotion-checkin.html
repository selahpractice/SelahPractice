Inside the Feeling

A five-step check-in for the moment a feeling is louder than your thoughts. Ground, name it, weigh it, trace it, let it speak — then hear what it needs.

Step 0 of 5 · Grounding
Be still, and know that I am God.Psalm 46:10

The Hebrew word is raphah — let your hands drop. Before we name anything, we arrive. Breathe with the circle: in as it grows, out as it settles. Three rounds is enough.

Step 1 — Name it

Feelings lose some of their volume when they get a name. Choose the closest one. Close is honest enough.

Step 4 — Let it speak

I pour out my complaint before him; I declare my trouble before him.Psalm 77:1 (paraphrased in the Selah reading)

Write it raw. Nobody reads this. It never leaves your browser.

And what do you need most right now?

Your reflection

A few ways to sit with this:

  • Lament — say it to God exactly as it is. A third of the Psalms do. It is a spiritual practice, not a failure of one.
  • The unsent letter — write what you would say if it were safe. Then close the book on it.
  • RAIN — Recognise it, Allow it, Investigate gently, Nurture what you find.
  • Physiological sigh — two short inhales through the nose, one long exhale through the mouth. The fastest known off-ramp for acute stress.
  • Prayer — not the polished kind. The honest sentence kind.
