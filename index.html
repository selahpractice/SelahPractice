About Your Practitioner

Peniel Eshetu

Integrative Health · Health Educator · MSW Candidate

Peniel's work has never fit in one room, so she stopped trying to make it fit.

She began in public health and health education. At CancerCare Manitoba, she did cancer prevention work at a provincial scale: teaching nutrition education, running smoking cessation programs, and carrying the Get Checked screening program into schools and communities across Manitoba. Her work has also taken her through Manitoba Health, Statistics Canada, Black Mental Health Canada, and Previvo Longevity Clinic, and to the front lines where health becomes survival: crisis lines, youth residential care, and overnight support for young people in hard seasons.

Underneath the résumé is the real credential. Peniel is a wounded healer. Chronic illness, a fractured relationship with the healthcare system, and a faith that had to learn how to limp taught her what no textbook covers: body, mind, soul, and spirit were never separate, and the church and the clinic each hold only half of what a hurting person needs. Her postgraduate training in clinical psychoneuroimmunology gave that conviction a science, showing how stress, immunity, and belief speak to one another.

Today, she is completing a Master of Social Work. In her practicum at RE/ACT Winnipeg, she serves as right hand to the Executive Director and runs the organisation's public voice. Alongside it, she is completing a Master of Health Science in Integrative Health, and serves on the board of Empower, a youth mental health initiative in Winnipeg's Ethiopian community.

She plays piano. She writes poetry and devotional work. She makes soap by hand. And she is building Selah Practice slowly, and on purpose: resources now, a book in the works, and a one-to-one practice that opens in 2028.

The Approach

Wrestling Beautifully

Selah's approach holds body, mind, soul, and spirit in one room, because in a real life they were never separate. Sacred grit: not faith that denies the body, and not science that denies the soul. It starts with an old story — scroll through it slowly.

Genesis 32 · The Night

Alone at the river

“And Jacob was left alone.”

Every wrestling starts here. The camp sent ahead, the river crossed, no one left to perform for. Just you, and the dark, and whatever has been following you.

Genesis 32 · The Wrestling

Hands full of God

“There wrestled a man with him until the breaking of the day.”

Not a metaphor first. A body, in the dark, holding on. Faith with its hands full — the kind you feel in your shoulders the next morning.

Genesis 32 · The Wound & the Name

Marked, and renamed

“Your name shall no longer be called Jacob, but Israel, for you have striven with God and with men, and have prevailed.”

He left with a blessing and a limp, and they were not opposites. The wound was not the failure of the story. It was the proof it happened.

Genesis 32 · The Sunrise

Peniel — the face of God

“The sun rose upon him as he passed Peniel, limping because of his hip.”

Peniel means face of God. It is the name she carries — and the ground this practice is built on: you can meet God in the wrestling, and walk out different. Blessing and limp, together.
