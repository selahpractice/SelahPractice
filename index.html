The Philosophy

Jacob walked away from the wrestling with a blessing — and a limp. Both were real.

Selah Practice holds body, mind, soul, and spirit in one room, because in a real person they were never separate. This is sacred grit: not faith that performs being fine, and not healing that leaves God out — but the slow, honest work of becoming whole, scar and all.

About Pennye

Pennye Eshetu

Integrative Health · Health Educator · MSW Candidate

Pennye's work has never fit in one room, so she stopped trying to make it. She began in public health and health education — including cancer prevention work at CancerCare Manitoba, where she taught nutrition education, ran smoking cessation programs, and carried the Get Checked screening program into schools and communities across the province. She has worked in community health with Manitoba Health, Statistics Canada, Black Mental Health Canada, and Previvo Longevity Clinic, and on the front lines where health becomes survival: crisis lines, youth residential care, and overnight support for young people in hard seasons.

Underneath the résumé is the real credential: she is a wounded healer. Chronic illness, a fractured relationship with the healthcare system, and a faith that had to learn how to limp taught her what no textbook covers — that body, mind, soul, and spirit were never separate, and that the church and the clinic each hold half of what a hurting person needs. Her postgraduate training in clinical psychoneuroimmunology gave that conviction a science: how stress, immunity, and belief speak to one another.

Today she is completing a Master of Social Work — her practicum at RE/ACT Winnipeg has her serving as right hand to the Executive Director and running the organisation's public voice — alongside a Master of Health Science in Integrative Health. She serves on the board of Empower, a youth mental health initiative in Winnipeg's Ethiopian community. She plays piano, writes poetry and devotional work, makes soap by hand, and is building Selah Practice slowly and on purpose: resources now, a book in the works, and a one-to-one practice that opens its doors in 2028.
