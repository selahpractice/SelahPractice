01

Wrestling Beautifully — the story

It starts with an old story. In Genesis 32, Jacob is left alone at the river at night, and someone wrestles with him until daybreak. He will not let go, and he leaves with a blessing and a limp — and they were not opposites.

“And Jacob was left alone.” — Genesis 32

Alone at the river. Hands full of God, holding on in the dark. Marked, and renamed. Then sunrise at Peniel — the face of God — limping, and blessed, both at once. The wound was not the failure of the story. It was the proof it happened.

“So Jacob called the place Peniel, saying, ‘It is because I saw God face to face, and yet my life was spared.’”
Genesis 32:30

Peniel means face of God. Jacob did not name a victory. He named the place where he wrestled — the ground where he held on through the night and came away blessed, renamed, and limping, all at once.

Peniel is the name I carry, and that is why this story sits at the centre of this practice. The blessing did not wait for the struggle to be over. It came in the struggle — in the wrestling, and in the formation that only wrestling does. You can meet God in it, and walk out different.

02

How it works

Pause — Selah, stop. Before fixing comes stopping. Neuroscience is clear that a nervous system stuck in survival cannot take in anything new, so I begin with regulation: breath, stillness, and safety first.

Attend — tell the truth. Lament before lessons. Psychology names what is actually happening — in the body, the story, and the faith — honestly, without rushing past it, because the brain changes where it is met, not where it is managed.

Integrate — put it together. Spirituality and science in one conversation. Through neuroplasticity, new experiences, repeated gently, lay down new pathways — so integration becomes a personal way of living you actually understand, not a pile of advice.

03

Who this is for

For people whose suffering does not fit in one box. Chronic illness that has rearranged who you are. A body stuck on high alert — panic, shutdown, exhaustion sleep does not fix. Helpers and caregivers who have given until something went quiet. And anyone whose faith has been strained by suffering, who needs a room where honest questions are not a spiritual failure.
