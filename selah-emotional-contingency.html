The Emotional Contingency Plan

When emotions are high and the urge is strong, you don't need more willpower — you need a plan. A plan you made when you were thinking clearly. Your emergency plan is your anchor: you write it down now, while you're calm, and you let it speak in the moment your feelings can't. Nothing here is stored or sent anywhere.

Step 1 of 5 · The moment

Step 1 — The moment

Everyone has a moment they can see coming — the surge, the reach, the point where the feeling starts driving. Name yours plainly, without judging it. A plan begins with honesty about where the moment finds you.

Or in your own words

Step 2 — The real need underneath

The urge is rarely about the thing itself. What are you actually craving? Name it.

Or name it your way

Step 3 — My response, decided ahead of time

Decide now, while you're thinking clearly. The shape of it is simple:

“When I feel ____, I will ____ instead.”

This is your strategy, not your struggle. Write two or three responses you are actually willing to do.

First, I will Then, I will And if it's still strong, I will

Step 4 — Make it visible

Put your plan on your fridge, bathroom mirror, or phone lock screen. If it's not visible, it's forgettable. Here it is, assembled — print it, save it, put it where the moment can find it.

    Step 5 — After: compassion, not shame

    If you slip, don't spiral. Practice self-compassion, not shame: a slip is a moment, not your identity. Gently return to the plan.

    And when you're ready, reflect — honestly, and without a verdict:

    • What does my body actually need in this moment?
    • What emotion is asking to be felt, not fed?
    • What would change if I responded to the need, not the urge?

    The lies that show up

    Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.Psalm 119:105

    In the moment, the urge rarely argues honestly. These are some of the lies that show up — and the truth that answers each one.

    The lie

    “You need it to cope.”

    The truth

    God's Spirit gives you power, love, and a sound mind — not panic or numbness.

    2 Timothy 1:7
    The lie

    “You'll never change. You're just an emotional eater.”

    The truth

    Your identity is not your struggle. You are a new creation in Christ.

    2 Corinthians 5:17
    The lie

    “This is too much. You can't handle it.”

    The truth

    You can do hard things through Christ who strengthens you. You've survived 100% of your worst days so far.

    Philippians 4:13
    Say it back
