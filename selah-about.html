About

Peniel Eshetu

I'm Peniel Eshetu. My work has never fit in one room, so I stopped trying to make it fit. I'm a health educator and a social work student building an integrative practice in Winnipeg — for people whose care has to hold body, mind, soul, and spirit together, because that's how a life actually works.

I hold a degree in health science and postgraduate training in clinical psychoneuroimmunology, and I'm completing master's studies in social work and in integrative health.

01

How I got here

I began in public health and health education. For years my work was cancer prevention and health education — teaching nutrition, supporting people to stop smoking, and carrying screening education into schools and communities. Alongside it, I worked in health education and research, always through an integrative lens: asking how the whole person fits into the numbers.

I have also worked on the front lines, where health becomes survival — on crisis lines, in youth residential care, and in overnight support for young people in hard seasons. That work changed how I understand care. A protocol only matters if it can reach a person at 2 a.m., in a body that is frightened and exhausted.

02

The wounded healer

Underneath all of it is the real credential: I am a wounded healer. I live with chronic illness. I know what it is to have a fractured relationship with the healthcare system — to be handed normal results while your body disagrees — and to hold a faith that had to learn how to limp.

That taught me what no textbook covers: body, mind, soul, and spirit were never separate, and the church and the clinic each hold only half of what a hurting person needs. My postgraduate training in clinical psychoneuroimmunology gave that conviction a science — a language for how stress, immunity, and belief speak to one another.

03

What I'm building

I'm building Selah Practice slowly, and on purpose. Right now, that means resources you can use today and a weekly letter. A children's book is in the works. And in 2028, Selah opens its doors to one-to-one practice.

I serve on the board of Empower, a youth mental health initiative in Winnipeg's Ethiopian community. I play piano. I write poetry and devotional work. And I make soap by hand — you'll find that maker's side of me in Selah Studio, the side door into this practice.

Peniel Eshetu
