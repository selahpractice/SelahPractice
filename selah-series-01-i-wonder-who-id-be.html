She Who Always Endures · Part 1 of 6

I Wonder Who I'd Be — If I Wasn't Always Enduring

There is a softer version of you somewhere. One who never had to be this tired, this guarded, this endlessly resilient.

6 min read · Identity · Resilience

She is not a stranger. She is you, in the life where nothing required you to become this strong.

She laughs easier. She rests without earning it. She lets people help her, not because she has finally collapsed, but because she never learned that needing people was a debt.

You became someone else to survive. That was survival, and it worked. But survival mode was never meant to be permanent. You were more before you had to be strong.

The version of you that armour built

The version of you that had to armour up, go numb, perform okay, keep moving — that version was protecting something underneath. She was not the problem. She was the perimeter.

But a perimeter is not a home. And somewhere along the way, the guarding became the personality, and the softness she was guarding started to feel like a rumour.

Heaven did not forget her. God sees what the armour was covering.The Selah Reading

Endurance is a season, not an identity

Scripture never calls endurance an identity. It calls it a season with an end: weeping may endure for a night. A night. Not a name. Not a life sentence.

This series is about the hidden cost of always being the strong one, and about the slow, holy work of letting the softer version of you come back into the room.

Start here: you do not have to grieve her and dismiss her in the same breath. She is allowed to be missed.
