Who Am I, Actually

12 min read - Pennye Eshetu - Selah Practice

I want to tell you who I am. Not the credentials version. The real version.

I spent most of my twenties in the healthcare system as a patient. Psoriasis. Gut issues no one could explain. Skin that kept revolting. ADHD I did not have language for until much later. A thyroid apparently within normal range while I was exhausted in ways sleep did not fix. All of it quietly co-occurring, quietly interconnected, quietly being missed.

I was studying public health while my own body was sending messages the healthcare system had no framework to read.

The Sick Care System

The system is not built to find you well. It is built to find you sick enough to treat. You come in with fatigue and skin flares and a gut that has not worked right in years, and if your bloodwork looks normal, you are sent home. Rarely with answers.

Our healthcare system is a sick care system. Extraordinarily good at acute intervention. Catastrophically poor at the chronic, the functional, the subclinical, the whole-person.

The autoimmune piece. The gut-skin axis. The ADHD so often undiagnosed in women, tangled up with hormones and stress. The thyroid that looks fine on paper. I had all of it. I had to become my own investigator.

The Dream That Started It

What I wanted originally was to become a naturopathic doctor. I wanted to be the practitioner who had time. Who read labs differently. Who asked about your sleep and your stress and your relationships and your faith and actually meant all of it. Naturopathic medicine was the first place I saw health treated as a whole-person project.

The path shifted. The degrees changed shape. But the dream did not.

The Moment Everything Connected

I first encountered psychoneuroimmunology deep into my health sciences training. PNI. The science of how the mind, nervous system, and immune system are not three separate systems but one continuous conversation.

Loneliness increases inflammatory markers at levels comparable to physical injury. Grief suppresses immune function. Chronic stress dysregulates the HPA axis in ways that affect thyroid conversion, gut permeability, skin barrier function, hormonal balance. The body keeps score of every unprocessed emotion, every year of running on empty.

My psoriasis was not just a skin condition. My gut issues were not just digestive. My ADHD was not just neurological. They were all speaking the same language. I just had not had the vocabulary to hear it.

Why This Practice Exists

I built Selah Practice for the woman who is where I was. Who has been to the doctor and been told everything looks fine. Who knows something is wrong but cannot get anyone to take the whole picture seriously.

I am not a finished product. Still in the degrees. Still managing my own health. Still wrestling. That is not a disclaimer -- it is the credential that matters most. I know this terrain from the inside.

What I Bring

I bring the BHSc, the public health training, the PNI postgraduate, the integrative health training, and the social work degree in progress. And I bring my own body. My own twenties. My own discovery that healing is not a destination -- it is a practice of turning toward yourself with the same care you give everyone else.

That is who I am. That is why Selah Practice exists. If any part of this sounds like your story -- you are in the right place.
