Your First Letter
Feet Like the Deer's

“The Sovereign Lord is my strength; he makes my feet like the feet of a deer, he enables me to tread on my high places.”
Habakkuk 3:19

Maybe you found this letter in the thick of it. Work that does not let up. A body that will not cooperate. People you love carrying things you cannot carry for them. An emotional load that feels steep and overwhelming, and a quiet question underneath it all: how am I supposed to keep standing on ground like this?

Here is what survival does to us. When the load gets steep enough, something in us tempts toward paralysis, or toward shutting down entirely. Psychology has a name for that, and it is not weakness, and it is not a lack of faith. It is a nervous system doing protective mathematics: when the threat feels bigger than our resources, the body conserves, numbs, freezes. It is not faithlessness. It is physiology.

But listen to what Habakkuk actually says about endurance. God-given endurance is not about dragging yourself forward through sheer grit. It is not white knuckles and a clenched jaw. It is a quiet, steady footing, given to you, on treacherous ground — feet made like the deer's, able to move through terrain that feels impossible, not because the terrain changed, but because the footing was supplied.

That means this, and let it land: your capacity does not have to be limitless. It was never the plan that you would be the limitless one. His presence undergirds every step, including the steps you take trembling, including the days your whole obedience is staying.

So tonight, lay down the need to fix or carry everything. You are allowed to stop mid-terrain. The deer does not apologise for the mountain, and she does not climb it in her own strength either. Rest is not the reward for finishing. Tonight, rest is how He restores your strength — while you sleep, in the dark, without your supervision.

A small practice: read the verse once more, slowly, out loud if you can. Then name the steepest part of your terrain in one honest sentence, and hand it over in one honest prayer. That is the whole assignment. The footing is His department.

Selah — pause, lift, look again.

— Pennye
