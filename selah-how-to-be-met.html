Selah · Being Met

How to be met.

Being met is not being fixed, managed, or talked out of pain. It is being approached with enough safety that the body does not have to protect itself from the person trying to help.

Being met is our part. The healing is God's.

Download the free PDF

I built Selah because too many hurting Christians are ministered to at a distance and then left alone in the long middle. Prayer matters. Scripture matters. Advice can matter. But a person can receive all three and still not feel met.

Meeting someone is slower than fixing someone. It asks: Where are you, really? What is this costing your body? What would help you not carry this alone?

01

Being met is not being managed.

Managed people get instructions. Met people get presence.

Management rushes to the lesson, the correction, the prayer, the solution. Meeting first notices the person: the shaking hands, the flat voice, the silence, the tears that will not come, the body that has gone still.

"Rejoice with those who rejoice; mourn with those who mourn." — Romans 12:15
02

What meeting someone looks like.

Come to where they are

Do not make the hurting person climb to your comfort level first. Move toward their reality.

Slow the pace

A threatened nervous system cannot receive much. Lower the demand. Let silence be allowed.

Believe the signal

Freeze, tears, numbness, irritability, and shutdown are information, not attitude problems.

Stay past the moment

Check in tomorrow. Remember next week. Duration is part of attunement.

The body regulates in connection. Safety cues — a steady voice, unhurried presence, choice, follow-through — tell the nervous system it does not have to fight alone.

03

What can retraumatize.

Retraumatizing care often sounds spiritual while it pressures the body. Watch for these:

Correcting before listening

Explaining someone's pain back to them before they feel heard teaches them to hide it better.

Rushing forgiveness

Forgiveness forced too early can become another place where the wounded person has to perform.

Calling freeze resistance

Freeze is protection. Shaming it makes the body protect harder.

Disappearing after intensity

Big prayer, big emotion, then silence can feel like abandonment with religious language on it.

Spiritual bypassing

"Just pray more" can skip the pain underneath. Sometimes a person needs presence before resurrection language.

Shame dressed as conviction

Shame says, "You are the problem." Conviction says, "Come home." They do not land the same in the body.

Also: praying over someone without consent, touching without asking, demanding the whole story, comparing suffering, or implying that continued pain means weak faith.

04

If someone freezes, do less — more gently.

Freeze can look like silence, blankness, delayed replies, or a person suddenly becoming very still. It does not always mean rejection. Often it means the system is overwhelmed.

"I'm here. We don't have to solve this right now."

"Do you want prayer, quiet, or help with one practical thing?"

"You don't have to explain it perfectly for me to stay."

Then honour the answer. Choice is part of safety.

05

A Christian practice of meeting.

James does not tell the sick person to crawl toward help. The elders come. Jesus stops for the woman who reached in secret. Scripture keeps showing the same movement: the well ones draw near, stay, and carry weight with the hurting.

"Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." — Galatians 6:2

This is not calling the church out. It is calling believers in — to activate faith as attunement. There is a science to being met, and it is written into the body itself.

06

When Christianity isn't trauma-informed.

Some Christian spaces are spiritually rich and emotionally unsafe at the same time. A person can love God and still leave smaller, more ashamed, or more alone than when they came in.

Trauma-informed Christianity makes room for slow forgiveness, boundaries, anger, questions, and the body. It remembers that Jesus wept before He raised Lazarus. It does not ask for performance first; it offers presence.

"The Spirit helps us in our weakness... the Spirit himself intercedes for us through wordless groans." — Romans 8:26

If church has ever felt unsafe, you are not weak for noticing. You may be aware. Faith can be rebuilt on safer ground — ground that holds space for your nervous system, your questions, your timeline, and your story.

Start here

Meet one another.

If you are the hurting one, you are not too much for needing to be met. If you are the helper, begin smaller than a solution: notice, slow down, ask, stay.
